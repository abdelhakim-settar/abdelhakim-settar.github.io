Research structure

Two themes, a scientific convergence.

Hydrogen-process intensification and biocomposite thermal degradation share a foundation in chemical kinetics, transport and modelling. Their interface opens a path towards thermochemical biomass conversion.

Theme 1 · H₂ processes ↗Theme 2 · Thermal degradation ↗Interface · Biomass ↗
01 · Gas–gas / heterogeneous catalysis

Catalytic methane steam reforming

Steam reforming involves gaseous reactants and heterogeneous catalysis. I investigate the interactions between chemical kinetics, heat supply and species transport in wall-coated catalytic reactors. The endothermic reaction directly connects temperature distributions, methane conversion and hydrogen production.

My contributions include spatial catalyst distribution, segmentation of active layers, metal-foam insertion and coupled-transport modelling. The objective is to understand physical limitations and intensification mechanisms, beyond a single yield metric.

02 · Solid–gas transformations

Thermal degradation and chemical kinetics

I study thermal decomposition mechanisms in composite and bio-based materials and their interactions with transport. TGA/DTG and DSC measurements provide information on mass loss, transformation stages and the effects of atmosphere and heating rate.

Kinetic analysis combines isoconversional methods — Friedman, KAS and OFW — with reaction models. It aims to identify parameters and mechanisms consistent with observations, then relate them to changes in properties and microstructure. Studies of natural fibres, resins and fillers address thermal stability, residue formation and transport in biocomposites.

03

Hydrodynamics and transport in porous media

Hydrodynamic and thermophysical properties determine the representativeness of reactor models. I combine metal-foam characterisation, permeability measurements and identification of Darcy–Forchheimer coefficients. These parameters then inform simulations of reactive flow and transport.

This approach connects material structure, pressure drop, temperature distributions and species balances. It extends to CFD model development, local thermal non-equilibrium and multiphase flows.

An integrated experimental and numerical approach

The common thread is the comparison of observations with models: measuring relevant properties, identifying parameters, solving coupled balances and analysing discrepancies to improve physical understanding.

Characterise

Permeability measurements, thermophysical properties, TGA/DTG, DSC and material characterisation.

Identify parameters

Chemical kinetics, activation energies, reaction laws and hydrodynamic coefficients.

Build models

Momentum, energy and species balances; fluid–solid coupling and porous-media models.

Validate and analyse

Measurement–simulation comparisons, parametric studies, sensitivity and interpretation of limiting mechanisms.

Current work & research directions

Convergence towards thermochemical biomass conversion

Biomass and biowaste pyrolysis and gasification provide a direction that connects my two themes: thermochemical reactor studies and the understanding of solid-degradation mechanisms. This builds on work in kinetics, transport and porous media, with hydrogen production as an application.

The work combines physicochemical characterisation, TGA/DTG, pyrolysis-product analysis and modelling. Data-driven approaches and machine learning complement physical models. HyBiOMICS aims in particular to connect material, particle and reactor scales for heterogeneous resources.
