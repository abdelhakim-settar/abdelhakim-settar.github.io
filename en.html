Research structure

Two themes, a scientific convergence.

Hydrogen-process intensification and biocomposite thermal degradation share a foundation in chemical kinetics, transport and modelling. Their interface opens a path towards thermochemical biomass conversion.

Theme 1 · H₂ processes ↗Theme 2 · Thermal degradation ↗Interface · Biomass ↗
Scientific approach

Connecting reactions, flow and transport.

My research investigates the interactions between chemical kinetics, heat and mass transfer, material properties and hydrodynamics. It is structured around two themes: catalytic methane steam reforming, and thermal degradation of composite and bio-based materials.

I build models using experimentally measured parameters, then compare their predictions with observations. Hydrogen production, reactor intensification and material thermal behaviour are applications of this approach.

Thermochemical biomass conversion is a research direction that brings these two themes together.

Sustainable development

Goals that give direction to my work.

My research and teaching address themes connected to these United Nations Sustainable Development Goals.

SDG 7Affordable and clean energy

Hydrogen production, thermochemical biomass conversion and improved transport in energy systems.

SDG 9Industry, innovation and infrastructure

Reactor intensification, multiphysics modelling and artificial intelligence for process design and optimisation.

SDG 12Responsible consumption and production

Biomass waste valorisation, bio-based materials and teaching in life cycle assessment.

SDG 13Climate action

Research on energy production pathways and process efficiency, with attention to decarbonisation challenges.

Education in energy engineering and sustainable construction, project-based learning and supervision of master’s and doctoral research.
